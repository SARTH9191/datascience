 Abstract— Financial Institutions must report Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) where any kind of suspicious activities that involve frauds or money laundering are noticed. However, the conventional method of drafting a SAR is highly manual and relies on compliance analysts. As a result, it becomes time-consuming and creates several operational and reporting challenges for the financial institutions. Moreover, with the increase in the volume of online banking transactions, there is a dire need to adopt intelligent and explainable automated solutions for Anti-Money Laundering (AML) purposes. This paper presents the idea of an Explainable AI-based SAR Narrative Generator that incorporates the use of RAG technology along with the audit trail approach for generating automated SAR reports. The proposed solution takes transaction alert data, KYC profile information of customers, and transaction history as the inputs. It utilizes a rule-based risk detection engine to detect suspicious transaction patterns including multiple deposits, fast fund movements, and international transactions. The relevant AML typologies, SAR templates, and regulatory guidance documents are identified via RAG pipeline based on the vector embeddings and ChromaDB. A Large Language Model (LLM) generates the regulator-ready SAR narratives using contextual and structured evidences. To ensure transparency in the process, the complete audit trail consisting of rules applied, knowledge sources utilized, transaction evidence, prompts, and generated SAR is maintained by the proposed solution. There is also a human-in-the-loop mechanism in the proposed solution for editing and approving SAR narrative drafts.

Keywords— Suspicious Activity Report, Anti-Money Laundering, Explainable AI, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Large Language Model, Audit Trail, Financial Compliance, Financial Crime Detection.

Introduction

The growth of digital banking, payment systems, and cross-border financial transactions has made it increasingly complex to monitor financial activity. Banks process large volumes of transactions every day, making it challenging for AML compliance teams to detect suspicious activity [1], [2]. Regulatory authorities require banks to submit Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) when abnormal transactions are identified. A SAR details the suspicious activity or behaviour of the customer, the transaction details, the evidence of the suspicious activity, and the rationale for identifying it as suspicious. Preparing SARs is a manual and time-consuming process. Analysts must review the transaction histories, customer profiles, activities, and risk factors before preparing a report. This increases the workload for the analysts, causes delays in reporting, and increases the possibility of inconsistent or incomplete reporting.

Artificial Intelligence, NLP, and LLMs present opportunities to automate financial compliance tasks and generate SAR narratives[6], [8], [9]. However, AI-based compliance systems have challenges such as hallucinations, lack of explainability, audit trails, and limited trust[3], [5], [8]. This paper proposes an Explainable AI-based SAR Narrative Generator that combines rule-based suspicious activity detection, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and audit trail capabilities. The system processes transaction alerts, KYC profiles, and account activity to identify suspicious activities such as multiple deposits from unrelated accounts, rapid fund transfers, and international transactions [1], [2].

The system uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline to retrieve relevant AML typologies, SAR templates, and regulatory guidance using vector embeddings and ChromaDB databases before generating the narrative using an LLM [5]. An audit trail records activated rules, evidence transactions, retrieved documents, system prompts, and outputs. The system operates Human-in-the-Loop functionality which enables compliance analysts to check and authorize the system-generated narratives. The system uses retrieval-based grounding together with explainability and human monitoring to decrease manual work while producing uniform SAR reports [4], [5], [10]. The system operates as a scalable solution which offers complete transparency to financial institutions for their AML regulatory compliance and financial crime reporting needs.

Literature review

    Due to the rapid growth of digital banking transactions, increasing financial fraud, and stricter regulations for financial compliance, there has been significant research into smart financial compliance and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) systems. This section reviews the research in six areas related to financial compliance and AML: AML transaction monitoring systems, explainable AI systems, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, Large Language Models in financial applications, NLP-based financial compliance systems, and human-in-the-loop AI systems.

A. Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Fraud Detection Systems.

Traditional AML systems use rule-based transaction monitoring to detect suspicious transactions. Studies have shown that machine learning, behavioural analytics, and customer profiling can enhance the detection of financial fraud and money laundering[1], [2]. These findings support the proposed system’s rule-based risk detection engine, which analyses customer behaviour and transaction patterns to identify suspicious activities.

B. Explainable Artificial Intelligence in Financial Systems

    Due to the regulations in financial systems, explainability is required for AI-based financial systems.[3] demonstrated that using Explainable AI (XAI) in financial risk management systems improves the transparency of those systems. [4] reviewed the explanation techniques for black-box AI models and introduced the need for interpretable reasoning in systems that operate within high-risk domains such as banking and financial compliance. These studies highlight the limitations of black-box AI systems in financial and compliance applications. The proposed financial compliance system utilizes these studies to justify the implementation of a feature that maintains the system’s audit trail, including the rules triggered, AML typologies retrieved, evidence transactions, and reasoning logs of the system.

C. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Knowledge Grounded AI 

RAG combines document retrieval with LLMs to improve factual accuracy and reduce hallucinations by providing domain-specific information[5]. The system retrieves relevant information from a vector database which includes AML typologies and SAR templates and regulatory guidance before it produces its reports. The system generates stories through official financial crime terminology which it obtains from regulatory documents instead of depending on the LLM's internal knowledge base.

D. Large Language Models in Financial Applications

The transformer-based Large Language Models were proven to create text that understands context by Brown et al. [6] in their few-shot learning capacity. [7] Introduced the transformer architecture, the foundation of current natural language generation (NLG) task-oriented LLMs. [8] explained how Large Language Models function in financial operations while showing their useful applications for regulatory reporting and compliance automation and financial report generation. The authors raised questions on hallucination and explainability and issues on Domain Adaptation. The system solves these problems by combining RAG-based contextual search with audit tracking for narrative creation and it uses particular prompts to guide LLM responses toward compliance work.

E. NLP-Based Compliance Monitoring Systems

Tasks like financial compliance and document creation are becoming more popular for the use of Natural Language Processing. The BERT model developed [9] brought major improvements to contextual language processing which led to the development of multiple NLP systems dedicated to compliance work. The newest methods of compliance monitoring involve NLP technology for developing summary statement reports on deal activity and additionally discovering the descriptions of suspicious behavior, and after that turning them into structured statement reports. The study confirms that the use of context-dependent knowledge and language generation techniques lowers the effort needed for compliance activities and improves the consistency of the generated files. The proposed system is based on these concepts, where the automated generation of Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) narratives creates a document that fulfills regulatory requirements by analyzing transaction records and customer KYC data and results from the AML typology database.

F. Human-in-the-Loop and AI-Assisted Compliance Systems

In high-risk environments such as financial services, human oversight remains essential. Vassallo et al. [10] proposed a Human-in-the-Loop machine learning approach for financial services and found that having analysts review the system significantly improved the trust and quality of decisions made by the AI-assisted system. The study presents a system which contains a human analyst review system that enables compliance officers to check and approve the SAR narrative content before submitting it. 

G. Research Gap

Research has explored different elements of AML transaction monitoring and explainable AI and RAG-based retrieval systems and Large Language Models but only a few studies have developed a unified system which generates SAR narratives. The current systems focus on specific areas which include detecting suspicious transactions but they fail to produce corresponding narrative reports. The system produces text through AI technology but it does not provide any information about its decision-making process. The system performs financial fraud classification but it lacks any form of auditor verification system. The system summarizes texts through NLP technology but it fails to meet the necessary requirements which regulatory authorities have established. The existing research fails to bring together the following elements which include rule-based AML transaction monitoring and retrieval augmented generation and explainable AI and audit trail logging and human analyst approval and regulator-ready SAR narrative generation. The system proposed in this study aims to fill this gap by developing an Explainable AI powered SAR Narrative Generator. The system produces Suspicious Activity Reports which contain basic information and clear format that meets regulatory standards through its automated process which analyzes transaction records and KYC data and AML patterns with Large Language Model support.

Methodology

A. System Overview

The proposed system functions as an Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) tool which generates Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) narratives to help organizations fulfill their Anti-Money Laundering (AML) compliance requirements. The system generates SAR narratives which regulators can use because it links rule-based risk detection with RAG and LLMs and audit trail systems to produce understandable results.  

The system receives three types of data which include transaction alerts together with customer KYC profiles and their complete account activity records. Suspicious transaction patterns are identified using predefined AML rules. The system extracts relevant AML typologies together with regulatory guidance through a RAG pipeline before the Large Language Model produces the final SAR narrative.

G. Discussion

    The experiment results show that Explainable AI, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and Large Language Models enhance the efficiency of SAR generation and its context relevance and transparency. The RAG module was able to articulate the generated narratives with the AML typologies and regulatory guidance effectively and thereby significantly reduced the hallucinated outputs typical of standalone LLM systems. The audit trail mechanism further enhanced the trust of the regulatory system since it was able to record detailed reasoning logs, along with mappings of evidence. Furthermore, the Human-in-the-Loop review function preserved ultimate compliance analysts' control over compliance reports generated, ensuring accountability and reliability in operations. In conclusion, the system proposed had a good potential to achieve automated AML reporting that was scalable, explainable and compliant with regulators.

Future Scope

The proposed SAR Narrative Generator could also be made even better by adding functionality that would allow the institution to monitor transactions in real time to detect suspicious activities and generate a SAR immediately. GNNs could be used to uncover any hidden transaction networks, mule accounts, circular transactions, and sophisticated layering patterns. Finance-focused large language models (LLMs) could contribute to better narrative generation and regulatory language. Adaptive ML-based risk scoring could be used to understand constantly evolving suspicious transaction patterns. Explainability could be improved through the use of SHAP and LIME techniques. The solution could be deployed through cloud-native architectures, microservices, Kubernetes, and scalable vector databases. Multilingual SAR generation could be considered for institutions that operate within different regulatory frameworks. Federated Learning could be used to collaborate with other financial institutions in a privacy-preserving manner. Integration with core banking systems, fraud monitoring systems, and case management solutions could help in creating end-to-end compliance workflows. Predictive compliance analytics could help in predicting any potential financial crimes, with the help of interactive explainability dashboards and continuous updates to the regulatory knowledge base.

 Conclusion

The suggested system uses Explainable AI to generate SAR reports which perform automated AML compliance functions. The system generates SAR reports through a combination of rule-based suspicious activity detection with RAG and LLMs and an audit trail system which produces clear and uniform SAR descriptions. The system performs customer profile analysis and transaction detail examination which helps to identify suspicious activities through its detection of multiple deposits and fast money transfers and worldwide operational patterns. The system uses RAG to find proper AML typologies and regulatory rules which help it build improved context that prevents hallucinations. The audit trail system keeps track of all rules which activated along with their corresponding evidence transactions and document retrievals and reasoning processes. Our system development through experimental research showed successful suspicious activity identification results and SAR reporting consistency and faster report generation times. Human-in-the-Loop allows for human analysis and approval of the created reports.
